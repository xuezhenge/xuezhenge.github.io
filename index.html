About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Wilfrid Laurier University. My research focuses on how climate change affects species interactions. I am currently working on two major projects.

With Professor Mike Bonsall (University of Oxford), I study climate change and vector‑borne disease. We recently finished a project on how climate shapes the efficacy of the sterile insect technique against Aedes aegypti, a globally significant disease vector. With Professor Jonathan Newman (Wilfrid Laurier University), I am modelling how climate change affects the interaction between grasses and their Epichloë fungal symbionts. I also have several active collaborations in China on species distribution modelling.

I completed my PhD in Ecology at the University of Guelph in 2023, where my thesis, Predator–Prey Interactions in a Warming World, used aphids and ladybird beetles to show how cold tolerance, seasonality and local adaptation decide whether predators keep pests in check as the climate warms. Before that I trained in forest conservation at Beijing Forestry University, modelling climate suitability for 104 invasive insects with CLIMEX.

Research

Climate and vector‑borne disease

Climate‑tuned models of sterile insect technique (SIT) releases for Aedes aegypti in Florida. They ask when to release sterile males so that suppression works best under current and future climates.

With M. B. Bonsall, Oxford · Ecological Applications, in review

Grasses and their fungal symbionts

Modelling how warming changes the interaction between grass hosts and their Epichloë endophytes, symbionts that protect grasses from herbivores and stress.

With J. A. Newman, Wilfrid Laurier · ongoing

Predators and prey in a warming world

Mechanistic population models that link thermal physiology to aphid–ladybird dynamics. They show how warming and seasonality weaken top‑down control and where evolutionary rescue is possible.

Functional Ecology 2023 · Evolutionary Applications 2024 · Journal of Biogeography 2024

Forecasting invasive pests

Ecoclimatic and niche models that project the global spread of forest and agricultural pests and their pathogens under climate scenarios, built to guide quarantine and surveillance.

Pest Management Science · Journal of Applied Ecology 2024 · PLoS ONE 2015

Climate risk to biodiversity people value

Projecting how climate change affects the wildflowers, pollinators and other species chosen as US state symbols, as a way to make climate impacts tangible.

People and Nature 2026

Reproducible ecology

Contributing analyst in a many‑analysts study showing how analytical decisions alone shift effect sizes in ecology and evolutionary biology.

BMC Biology 2025
